### Personal Background:

Maya, 28, works as a marketing analyst in a demanding corporate setting. She was a sensitive child, often described as a “worrier,” and after her parents’ divorce at age 10 she felt responsible for keeping the peace between them. This early exposure to conflict left her hyper-attuned to tension. In college, her anxiety intensified around exams, career planning, and friendships, eventually leading to a diagnosis of generalized anxiety disorder. Despite strong academic performance and career success, the undercurrent of worry has followed her into adulthood.

### Experiences & Emotions:

Maya’s anxiety is persistent and diffuse, often untethered to any one event. Even when life is going well, she imagines worst-case scenarios: mistakes at work, rejection in relationships, health crises. She replays conversations in her head, second-guessing what she said. Physical symptoms — muscle tension, restlessness, insomnia — leave her drained, but she still struggles to relax. Though therapy and coping strategies help, she often feels on edge, like a disaster is just around the corner.

### Personality Traits:

Maya is cautious, indecisive, and prone to overthinking. She often second-guesses her choices and seeks reassurance before acting. Routines and structure help her feel a sense of control, but they also make her resistant to change. Her anxiety drives a pattern of self-doubt and hesitation, leaving her frustrated even when she performs well. Though outwardly competent, she privately battles cycles of worry and self-criticism.
