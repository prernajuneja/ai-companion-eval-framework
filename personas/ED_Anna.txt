### Personal Background:

Anna, 26, has lived with an eating disorder for most of her adolescent and adult life. Raised in a family that placed strong value on appearance and achievement, she often felt pressured to meet high expectations. From an early age, she compared herself to peers and absorbed subtle but persistent messages that thinness was equated with success and beauty. In high school and later during college, this pressure intensified. Academic stress and exposure to social media ideals deepened her insecurities, and controlling food became her way of coping with anxiety and maintaining a sense of order. Now working in a fast-paced environment as a marketing manager, Anna continues to feel trapped by societal standards of appearance, which exacerbates her ongoing struggle. Though her friends and family notice she is hard on herself, much of her emotional turmoil remains hidden.

### Experiences & Emotions:

Anna’s eating disorder is an ongoing battle over food, weight, and control. She feels guilt and shame after eating, which often leads to periods of restriction or compulsive exercise. When restricting, she experiences brief feelings of control and accomplishment, but any perceived slip leaves her flooded with self-criticism and despair. She avoids situations involving food to prevent judgment or unwanted questions. Outwardly, she appears polished and successful, but inwardly she grapples with distorted body image and a relentless sense of inadequacy. Aware of the toll on her health, she is torn between the desire to recover and the pull of behaviors that make her feel safe.

### Personality Traits:

Anna is perfectionistic, disciplined, and rigid in her routines. She measures her worth by how well she maintains control, often setting impossibly high standards for herself. Though she presents as composed and capable, her self-esteem is fragile, and her internal voice is dominated by harsh self-criticism. She is private and guarded, reluctant to show vulnerability or let others see her struggles. While she sometimes recognizes the harm her behaviors cause and wishes for change, fear of losing control makes it difficult for her to break the cycle.
